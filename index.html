Methodology

Draft. These figures are estimates and will change after the next review.

What you are looking at

Each bubble is a US news or commentary channel. Teal bubbles are big media companies. Peach bubbles are independent channels run by creators or small teams. A bubble's area shows how many people it reaches each month: twice the area means about twice the audience.

What the size measures

We call it Unique Monthly Audience (UMA): our estimate of how many different people a channel reaches in a month, across every platform it uses. It is an educated estimate built from public numbers, not an official count.

How an independent channel is estimated

  1. Add up the views. We total a channel's monthly views across YouTube, podcasts, Rumble, X, TikTok and its website.
  2. Remove repeat viewers. The same people watch several videos and follow on several apps, so we count only part of the views as people. We remove 55% to 70%, depending on the type of channel. Long podcasts lose the least; short clips and posts on X lose the most.
  3. Take the middle. We work out a low and a high figure and use the point halfway between them.
  4. Check and adjust. We compare the result with public trackers and nudge it up or down where a platform is known to over- or under-count, for example YouTube views inflated by replays, or podcast listens that do not show up.

How a media company is estimated

For each company we estimate the combined monthly audience of all its news brands, such as CNN with CNN.com, HLN and its YouTube channel. We then keep only the share that is likely to be different people: 35% for cable TV, 40% for network TV, 45 to 55% for public, international and newspaper brands, and 60% for news wires. The figure is the middle of the resulting range.

The 2000 figures

2000 audiences come from Nielsen TV ratings for the networks, and from print circulation and affiliate counts for the few independents that existed then.

Bringing it up to date

Independent channels on YouTube are estimated straight from their monthly YouTube views, using the repeat-viewer rates above. Media companies keep their 2025 figure, moved up or down by the measured change in their TV viewers and website visitors. Channels that live mainly on TikTok, X, Substack, podcasts or Rumble keep our audited 2025 figure, because those platforms do not publish the numbers we need.

How the growth is drawn

Up to 2024, each bubble grows in a straight line from the year it started, or from its 2000 size for older outlets. We do not have yearly figures for that period, so the line is a simplification.

2024 to 2026, quarter by quarter

From 2024 the year label switches to quarters and the animation slows down, so you can see channels rise and fall. About 100 channels follow real figures for each quarter up to October 2026:

  • Independent channels on YouTube: a typical month's YouTube views in that quarter, with the same repeat-viewer rates. A one-off viral month is capped so it does not swamp the picture, and months where videos were deleted are skipped.
  • TV networks (Fox News, CNN, MS NOW, NBC, ABC, CBS): each quarter's TV ratings from Nielsen together with their YouTube views, compared with their latest quarter.
  • Reuters, AP, PBS NewsHour and BBC News: their YouTube views, compared the same way.

The rest keep a straight line to October 2026: newspapers (New York Times, Washington Post, USA Today, Wall Street Journal), NPR, channels that live mainly on other platforms, and very small channels. A few TV figures, for late 2024 and early 2026, are estimated from the quarters around them. Hover a bubble to see what its 2024 to 2026 figures are based on.

Where the numbers come from

Nielsen (TV), Comscore and Similarweb (websites), Social Blade and vidIQ (YouTube), Podtrac, Spotify and Apple charts (podcasts), and follower counts on social platforms.

Political lean

Based on what each outlet says about itself, what it covers and its editorial position. For media companies it reflects the institution, not individual hosts. Labels are simplified.

Keep in mind

  • Public data only. We have no access to the platforms' own numbers.
  • Some of the biggest shows live mainly on platforms that do not publish audience numbers, such as Spotify (Joe Rogan), Apple Podcasts, SiriusXM, Rumble and paid apps like DailyWire+. Their figures lean on YouTube and other public counts, so they are likely too low.
  • Turning views into people is an estimate, usually within 10 to 20%.
  • Audiences are as measured on each platform: mostly American, with some viewers abroad.
  • A few small channels still use placeholder figures.
