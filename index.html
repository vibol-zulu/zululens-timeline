Methodology

What you are looking at

Each bubble is a US-based news or commentary outlet reaching at least 500K people a month. Corporate outlets are owned by media companies and run by institutional newsrooms, mostly rooted in TV, print or radio. Independent channels are run by individual creators or small teams who own their brand, mostly online-first. The bigger the bubble, the bigger its monthly audience.

How we size a bubble

We estimate how many different people each outlet reaches in a month, using public numbers.

Independent channels

YouTube views × assumed audience share

Legacy media built their reach on TV, a visual medium. YouTube is its closest equivalent for independents, and the only platform with consistent public numbers. This formula doesn’t count audio-only listening on Spotify and Apple. For the biggest podcast-led shows we estimate it separately from other research (see Outer circles below); other podcast-led shows may still be understated.

The share (47–59%, depending on the type of channel) is meant to remove people who watch more than one video.

This share is an assumption, not a measured figure. No platform publishes how many of a channel’s views come from different people. Our percentages come from the 2025 research, where they were set by judgement, with no published source. The idea: formats people watch habitually (daily shows, live streams, X posts) get more repeat views, so a smaller share counts as people; one-off long episodes (podcasts, interviews) get fewer, so a larger share counts. The only public measurement we found (Comscore, 2013) suggests the real share may be lower, so independent audiences may be overstated.

Large YouTube figures are likely too high. Channels that post many videos a day, like MeidasTouch, get heavy repeat viewing from loyal fans, and YouTube counts Shorts as views too, so far fewer of their views are different people. Independent rankings agree: Edison puts MeidasTouch 20th in US weekly podcast reach, below several channels that look smaller here. Channels above 40M on YouTube carry this note. Each YouTube-sized channel’s tooltip also shows how many videos it posts a month, its share of Shorts and its average views per video, so you can see when a big figure comes from posting volume.

Example: MeidasTouch had 228.6M YouTube views in October 2026. As a news commentary channel, 53.5% count as people: 228.6M × 0.535 ≈ 122M.

Media companies

Combined audience × share kept

We add up all of a company’s news brands, keep the share that is likely different people (35–60%), and use the middle of the range.

Example: Fox News reaches 146–176M a month in total. Keeping 35% gives ≈ 56M.

Outer circles

For six of the biggest podcast-led shows (Joe Rogan, Candace Owens, Tucker Carlson, Ben Shapiro, Theo Von and Jordan Peterson), the solid circle is their YouTube audience and the fainter outer circle includes podcasts and other platforms. The outer figure comes from our 2025 cross-platform research, moved by their change in YouTube views.

Pod Save America also has an outer circle, using Podscribe’s measured audio audience (5.0M monthly listeners, October 2026) on top of its YouTube audience.

Independent 2026 rankings back this up: Edison Research ranks Joe Rogan 1st and Theo Von 7th in US weekly podcast reach across all platforms (Q3 2026), well above several channels that look bigger on YouTube alone.

Over time

Up to 2024, each bubble grows in a straight line from its launch, or from its 2000 size. From 2024, about 100 outlets follow real quarterly figures. Hover a bubble to see what its figures are based on.

Sources

Nielsen (TV), Comscore and Similarweb (websites), vidIQ and Social Blade (YouTube).

Political lean

Based on how each outlet describes itself and what it covers. Labels are simplified.

Keep in mind

  • These are estimates from public data, not official counts.
  • Independent figures depend on an assumed share of views that are different people. If the real share is lower, those bubbles are too big.
  • Podcast apps, Rumble and paid apps don’t publish audiences, so some shows, like Glenn Beck’s (radio and BlazeTV), are likely too low.
  • Scripps News, OAN and Real America’s Voice have no Nielsen ratings, so they are sized on YouTube only.
  • “US” means where an outlet is based, not where its audience lives. Audiences are counted as each platform measures them, so YouTube figures include viewers outside the US.
